Reading — the unified queue

Consolidated 26 Aug 2026 from _master-report/READING-own-work-dependencies.md, ml-research/reading/reading-list-mynbaev-ml.md, the reference lists of all three KASE papers, and lw-shrinkage-artifact/TARGETS.md. Thirty-six papers. At one a day that is about five weeks.

⚠ Honest calibration before you start. One paper a day is 365 a year, and a Tier-1 statistics paper read properly is 45–90 minutes. That does not survive alongside a 20 h/week study floor unless most days are targeted 30-minute reads rather than full comprehension passes. The realistic failure mode is not laziness: it is reading ten tomorrow and none after. So the queue is tiered by depth, the streak pauses during the May 2027 and May 2028 sittings, and a 20-minute skim that answers the four questions counts as a day.

What "read" means here. Not cover to cover. For each paper you want four things: (1) what question it answers, (2) what it assumes, (3) what happens when the assumption fails, (4) the one result you cite, and whether you cite it correctly. Write those four lines down. If you cannot write line 3, you have not read it.

Queue A — the defensibility five TOMORROW

These are the five methods in your own papers you would be asked about first, by Aldashev, by a summer-programme reviewer, or in an interview. Until these are done the KASE repos are a liability rather than an asset.


  • Every t-statistic in your paper is now HAC. Know what it corrects for and why lag 5 is defensible.

  • One of your two spread estimates. Rests on serial covariance of price changes; know when the assumption fails.

  • Your other estimate. The two disagree by a factor of two in your data and you will be asked why.

  • You explicitly rejected this method. State its contemporaneous-observation assumption and why the KSPI pair violates it.

  • Your paper reports it as specification-fragile for the non-overlapping pair. Know what the permanent-transitory decomposition does.

Getting the PDFs

  • KBTU library proxy first, and check separately what the University of London subscription gives you — EMFSS students often have access the KBTU login does not cover.
  • Author preprints. Wolf's UZH page carries the Ledoit–Wolf papers openly. Mynbaev's are nearly all free on MPRA; the 2017 one is on arXiv.
  • Google Scholar "All versions" surfaces open PDFs the main result hides. This works more often than the paywall suggests.
  • SSRN and NBER for the finance papers.
  • For the iPad: pull them into one folder and sync once, rather than fetching per day. The friction of finding the PDF is what kills a daily habit, not the reading.
