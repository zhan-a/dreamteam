What the handoff brief undersold

Read from the repo itself (507 commits, ~/Documents/trading desk/) — three things materially stronger than PUBLISHABLE.md claims:
  • There is a natural experiment sitting unused. The brief frames candidate 2 as "a non-overlapping cross-listing". In fact four dual-listings were studied in the same market, under the same broker, costs and currency — and they split by session overlap: KSPI/NASDAQ = zero overlap (2.5 h gap), while KZAP/LSE and AIRA/LSE overlap by 4 hours. Comparing price-discovery shares across overlap regimes with the venue held constant is a far stronger design than the single-pair study, and it is already in the data. This is the single biggest upgrade available to the paper.
  • A second publishable finding is buried in the summary: arbitrage decay. The KZAP/LSE premium runs +48.4% (2019) → +27.9% (2022) → +10.2% (2024) → +0.6% (2026) — seven years of a frontier dual-listing premium being competed away. That is a clean, self-contained story about arbitrage capital entering a frontier market, and it needs no new analysis.
  • Groundwork already exists that the brief lists as "remaining work". Cointegration is tested (ADF p=0.005, half-life 2.5 days), Granger causality is run (p=0.000), and a price-discovery split is already computed (NASDAQ 68% / KASE 32%). Also present and rarely done well by undergraduates: an interest-rate-parity adjustment showing ~8.57%/yr of the observed premium is fair compensation (KZT 14% vs USD 5%), not alpha.
Implication for the schedule: candidate 2 is closer to a paper than the brief's "2–3 weeks of econometrics" suggests — but the right work is the overlap-vs-non-overlap comparison, not chasing Hasbrouck. Still an October-onward task.

Three open methodological points

  • Probably do NOT chase Hasbrouck information shares. They assume both venues observe a common efficient price contemporaneously; the whole point of this setup is that the sessions don't overlap, and standard IS on non-synchronous data gives wide, muddy bounds. Position against the non-overlapping / overnight-transmission spillover literature instead, where return-spillover regressions are the standard tool. The strict sequencing (NASDAQ → KASE → NASDAQ) supplies a natural recursive ordering — an identification advantage worth highlighting, not a gap to apologise for. A daily VECM + Gonzalo–Granger is defensible and far cheaper than the budgeted plan.
  • The 17%-risk-free hurdle has a peso problem. Measuring tenge depreciation over 2019–2026 excludes the 2015 devaluation; high local nominal rates are the price of exactly that jump risk. A blocked deposit also isn't usable capital, and insurance caps expose bank credit risk. Direction of the error: the hurdle is overstated, so "nothing clears it" is too strong as written — though nowhere near enough to rescue Sharpe 0.30 ± 0.66.
  • Sharpen the non-exploitability claim. First-print entry returns +0.856% against 0.65% cost — marginally positive. The precise statement is "not exploitable by a participant who cannot obtain queue priority against a stale market-maker quote", which is a more interesting sentence than a flat negative.

Guardrails — claims that must not be made

  • Not "rare data" — the contribution is assembly, validation and documentation, never acquisition.
  • No strategy is profitable or deployable. The year-end effect is descriptive.
  • Never cite the 526bp figure; cite Roll 0.24% / Corwin–Schultz 0.47% as estimates. No true top-of-book exists, so the spread remains unmeasured.
  • The transmission result is a measurement, not an arbitrage opportunity.
  • No novel methodology is claimed — the contribution is the venue, the resolution and the care.
  • Do not redistribute the raw bars without permission; keep the data-source description neutral and unsensational.
